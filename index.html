03Teaching Philosophy

Inquiry over recall.
Evidence before assertion.

Explore philosophy & practice ↗

A pedagogical approach shaped by formal teacher education, school observation, and the conviction that students understand history when they investigate it like historians.

01 FOUNDATIONAL INQUIRY

Inquiry before recall

I begin with a question, source or puzzle so that dates and events become evidence in an explanation—not isolated facts to memorise.

Classroom Implication: Students examine primary sources, maps, and contradictory accounts before dates and timelines.
02 DIFFERENTIATED PATHWAYS

Different routes, shared depth

Scaffolded prompts, visual sources, discussion and deeper comparison help learners reach the same meaningful understanding regardless of starting points.

Classroom Implication: Flexible comprehension routes balance textual analysis, visual interpretation, and structured dialogue.
03 FORMATIVE FEEDBACK

Assessment that changes teaching

Short checks, source-based tasks and specific feedback reveal what learners understand and what the next lesson must address.

Classroom Implication: Diagnostic checks determine whether to reteach with fresh scaffolding or advance to synthesis.

Educator Reflection

“Good teaching starts with a good question.”

History is not a ledger of completed dates; it is the discipline of asking how we know, whose voice is recorded, and what evidence justifies our claims. When students learn to weigh sources critically, they gain confidence not only in the classroom, but as citizens.
